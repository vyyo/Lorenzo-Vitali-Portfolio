mEat Gala

Overview
Analysis

Foreword

The main goal with mEat Gala was to design a game that humorously conveyed “l’ appel du vide”: an irrational urge to engage in self-destructive behavior. To recreate the feeling, the game meddles with the player’s input. As one of two designers and the project’s programmer, I’ve designed and coded these aspects of the game:

Core

  • Conflict: mastering uncomfortable controls, timing
  • Goal: eating all courses
  • The player’s hands automatically try reaching for food
  • If the ladies catch the player eating, the crowd closes in
  • The crowd is too close/the stomach fully empties over time = game over

Courses/Food

A course is a randomly generated and positioned set of foods. The course is complete when all of its food is eaten. Each food has a set of custom parameters:

  • Course: the course it can appear in
  • Shape: the size of the food’s hitbox
  • Value: how much it refills the stomach, when eaten
  • Input: the input type required to eat it
  • Resistance: how many times the input has to be repeated

Controls: Hands

The hands' controls are meant to communicate restlessness and an uncontrollable hunger. The player moves them with the left stick, however:

  • If left unattended, they automatically try reaching for food
  • Once they touch any food, they won’t move until it’s been fully eaten
  • When the stomach is less than 65% full, hand controls are inverted

Controls: Eating

Eating food is the only way of refilling the stomach and completing a course. Eating controls are meant to mimic the experience of stuffing yourself full:

  • Once the hands touch a food, eating starts
  • Mashing the displayed input gradually lowers the food’s resistance
  • Depleting the resistance results in eating the food, freeing hand movement

Ladies/Crowd

Each time the ladies seated in front of the player catch them eating, they call the crowd closer, creating a sense of judgement and impending (social) doom. Their movement logic aims to create a sense of unpredictability and tension:

  • The longer they look to the side, the more likely they are to chat
  • When chatting, they can either look to the side or forward (likelier over time)
  • Once they look at you (forward), the movement logic resets
