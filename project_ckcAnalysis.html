Foreword

The main goal with Cyber Kill Chain was to create a heavily gameplay-focused demo, capable of showcasing that each team member had learned “the ropes”, during their years as students. As the project's team leader and lead designer, I've worked on these aspects of Cyber Kill Chain:

Core

  • Conflict: understanding room layouts and destroying enemies carefully
  • In order to progress, gate opening conditions must be met
  • These conditions usually require destroying protected enemies
  • Protected enemies are made vulnerable through indirect infection, by using the virus to infect environment elements

Infection

  • To infect, the player shoots and maneuvers its head like a remote control missile, planting it in a port
  • When a port is infected by the virus, things connected to it will also be contaminated
  • The infection spreads upon physical contact between infected things and enemies or net creatures
  • The emissive parts of an infected element match the protagonist’s colors

Controls

To keep the focus on solving action-puzzles, the game’s controls are minimal, incentivizing the mastering of a few simple actions:

  • Semi-automatic Attack (Trojan): left stick; touch an enemy while moving
  • Remote Control Missile (Virus): button east, then left stick to maneuver
  • Plant/Unplant Led: button south, near a plant point

Gates

There are two types of gates, each emphasizing a different playstyle:

  • Counter gates: eliminate enemies until the counter hits 0 (action-puzzle)
  • Pacifist gates: eliminating any enemy closes the gate (stealth-puzzle)

Rooms

  • Each room is a self-contained puzzle
  • Whenever a player leaves a room, it resets to its default state, aside from major player progress (environment infections, opened counter gates)
  • This prevents softlocks and maintains a level of challenge if the player backtracks (puzzles stay solved, enemies respawn); while not feeling like a real reset

Enemy Design Philosophy

All creatures have simple behaviors, focused on creating dynamics with both player skills and the environment. They are conceived as interactive puzzle pieces

Enemies

  • Aggressive creatures (contact damage)
  • Enemies follow a predetermined path
  • If the player enters their fov, they start chasing within a delimited area
  • Only infectable through indirect means

Leds

Leds behave like fragile interactive keys the player has to escort:

  • Allied creatures
  • Targeted by enemies
  • Infected by proximity
  • When infected, they follow the player
  • Planting them in a socket deactivates any connected barriers

Net Creatures

Net creatures simultaneously serve as obstacles and useful tools for the player:

  • Neutral creatures
  • Net creatures follow a predetermined path
  • They instantly pick up nearby leds, carrying them over gaps
  • They can act as viral carriers, infecting enemies upon contact

Enemy: Arms

“Tutorial enemies” that explain the boss’ weak point, while testing the player’s mastery of virus maneuvers, leds and barriers:

  • Doesn’t move: it looks around instead
  • If the player enters their fov, they shoot their hand
  • If their hand hits a barrier, it falls and can be infected directly

Enemy: Boss

Verifies all player skills, while adding an element of dexterity:

  • Action puzzle, with a less rigid solution than previous levels
  • Aggressive, but easy to bait

Level Design Philosophy

Levels are made out of modular elements, making them both easier to read for the player and faster to build and test for the team. The player is slightly smaller than a single square on the map.

Level 1

  • Introduces: abilites, base and protected enemies, counter and pacifist gates, checkpoints and HP+
  • A high-density level instead of a tutorial, designed for live playtesting at events
  • Offers both a simpler (mandatory) route, and two optional routes (HP+)

Level 2

  • Introduces: interactions and applications of netstars and leds
  • Mainly focuses on puzzles and timing, combat is secondary
  • Features an optional, more complex puzzle (HP+)

Level 3

  • Introduces: arms
  • Focuses on combat exclusively
  • Short, linear and preparatory to the boss
  • Action feeling: harder, more fluid

Boss Arena

  • The arena makes use of all previously mastered mechanics
  • The boss allows for a multitude of approaches: dexterity, taking advantage of the arena’s layout or finding flaws in the boss’ AI

Overworld

  • The interactive and explorable level select makes the experience more immersive
  • Each shortcut recalls the previous level’s theme
  • Concentric layout: like a malware, the player infiltrates deeper into the game’s world
