The AdBlocker

Analysis

Overview
Analysis

Foreword

The main goal with The AdBlocker was to create a charismatic vertical slice, while keeping it simple enough for a team of first-timers. The game is also a study on harnessing annoyances into a core experience component. As the project's team leader and lead designer, I've worked on these aspects of The AdBlocker:

Core

  • Conflict: going unnoticed, despite being easily detectable due to the pop-ups
  • Goal: reaching the elevator, without anybody ringing the alarm
  • Enemies that see/hear the player will try ringing the alarm (game over)
  • Nearby transmitters bombard the player with pop-ups
  • Pop-ups are both annoyances and tools to distract enemies (through sharing)

Transmitters

Transmitters are the only way of obtaining pop-ups. As such, they become a tool to establish mid-level goals and to harness the annoyance created by pop-ups:

  • Each transmitter’s Area of Effect’s color matches the emitted pop-up type
  • Entering a transmitter’s AoE populates the screen with pop-ups over time
  • Green/red share icon = shareable/non-shareable pop-ups

Pop-Ups

  • When near a transmitter, pop-ups gradually populate the screen
  • Mandatory tools that incapacitate enemies (sharing)
  • Different annoyances and share targets for each type
  • Only removable by clicking their close and share buttons
  • Diegetic UI elements and storytelling tools

Pop-Up Type: Visual

Blue pop-ups, with the following properties:

  • Visual annoyance: parts of the screen are covered
  • Movement annoyance: movement is impossible in the covered areas
  • Share: targets and distracts an individual worker enemy

Pop-Up Type: Auditory

Purple pop-ups that have the same annoyances as a Visual pop-up, plus:

  • Stealth annoyance: audible to nearby enemies, without direct eye contact
  • Share: targets a speaker, distracts all workers in its range

Pop-Up Type: Physical

Yellow pop-ups that have the same annoyances as a Visual pop-up, plus:

  • Movement annoyance: can’t cross narrow paths due to the bigger hitbox
  • Stealth annoyance: more visible due to the bigger hitbox
  • Share: targets a platform, generating an item that halts roamers on contact

Controls

Controls exclusively rely on the left mouse button, simulating the experience of managing pop-ups on a browser tab:

  • Movement: left click, on any destination not covered by a pop-up
  • Rearranging pop-ups: hold left click, then drag the pop-up
  • Closing pop-ups: left click, on its close button
  • Sharing pop-ups: left click, on its share button, then aim + left click on a compatible target within reach

Enemies

Enemies will try to ring the alarm if they hear the player, or their FoV is entered. The game has two complementary enemy types:

  • Workers: sedentary; distracted by Visual and Auditory pop-ups
  • Roamers: regular patrol path; halted on contact with Physical pop-ups

Stealth

To go unnoticed without sharing, the player can:

  • Avoid entering enemy Fields of View
  • Keep enemies far enough from the sound of Auditory pop-ups
  • Use stealth carpets to ignore enemy FoVs (audio isn’t nullified)

Level Design Philosophy

Due to the prototype being a vertical slice, each level is part of a somewhat steep skill chain. Levels are made out of modular elements, simplifying the blockout process and improving readability for the player

Level 1

Diegetic tutorial:

  • Introduces: base stealth mechanics, first shareable pop-ups, Visual pop-ups, worker enemies

Level 2

The player starts independently looking for shareable pop-ups:

  • Introduces: non-shareable pop-ups, multiple paths

Level 3

Teaches the importance of using the correct pop-up type:

  • Introduces: Auditory pop-ups

Level 4

Focus on working around the sound area of Auditory pop-ups

Level 5

The new enemy type introduces dynamic elements for the first time:

  • Introduces: Physical pop-ups, roamer enemies

Level 6

Final test, combining worker and roamer enemies
