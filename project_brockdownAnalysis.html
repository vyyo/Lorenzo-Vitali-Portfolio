Brock Down

Analysis

Foreword

The main goal with Brock Down was to design a game that revolved around its fail-state. This was also a study on “gamified dialogue”, that impacted gameplay and in-game resources. The result is a dual-genre game, with its halves communicating in-between failures. As a solo dev, I focused on these aspects of Brock Down:

Core

  • Conflict: neutralizing/preventing breakdowns while aiding chore completion
  • Each chore requires a specific emotion to be completed
  • Dialogues modify the amount of stored emotions
  • If Brock exceeds his emotional capacity, he has a breakdown (near-fail state)
  • Neutralizing breakdowns through shooter segments lowers stored emotions

RPG

The game is a set of walking-sim RPG scenes, containing chores completed by:

  • Walking around
  • Interacting with objects
  • Talking to NPCs (linear and branching dialogues)

RPG: Emotions

An emotion is dominant when its stored amount is the highest. Each dominant emotion unveils unique interactions and dialogues, necessary for chore completion:

  • Rage: brute force; violent replies
  • Fear: higher walk speed; humble replies
  • Sadness: water generation (tears); pitiable replies
  • Joy: pain immunity; empathic replies

Breakdown

Breakdowns are near-fail states, entered when the sum of Brock’s emotions surpasses his emotional capacity. They give the player a chance to simultaneously avoid a game over and lower stored emotions.

Breakdowns effectively become the game’s resource management tool, as they are the only way to reliably change the dominant emotion

Shooter

Entering a breakdown starts a twin stick shooter segment:

  • Different types of EmoClots spawn, proportionally to stored emotion amounts
  • Eliminating an EmoClot lowers the corresponding emotion
  • Not lowering the emotions below capacity in time/reaching 0 HP = game over
  • Breakdowns become progressively shorter

Shooter: EmoClots

Each emotion corresponds to an EmoClot enemy type. EmoClots are damaged by opposite-type bullets, and powered up by same-type bullets. Each enemy is unique:

  • Rage: red; opposite: fear; chases slowly; punches frontally
  • Fear: green; opposite: rage; runs away; leaves a tray of toxic, retarding gas
  • Sadness: blue; opposite: joy; keeps a distance; shoots tears
  • Joy: yellow; opposite: sadness; moves to a random spot; explodes on arrival

Shooter: Weapons

Each emotion has its corresponding weapon and button. To keep inputs intuitive, buttons east-west and north-south are complementary bullets. Each weapon is unique:

  • Rage: button east; opposite: fear; short range band-aid shotgun
  • Fear: button west; opposite: rage; AoE gas around the player
  • Sadness: button south; opposite: joy; long-range, high-ratio pill projectiles
  • Joy: button north; opposite: sadness; exploding firework missile

Prototyping

In order to grow accustomed to the Godot game engine, I made everything from scratch, aside from the dialogue system (Dialogic plugin)
